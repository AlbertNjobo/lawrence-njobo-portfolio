BloodChain

A blood supply traceability system for Zimbabwe that keeps personal data private and the audit trail public.

Role
Final year project, NUST
Stack
React, TypeScript, Express, Supabase, Hedera Token Service, FastAPI
Status
Submitted 2026, marked 81, first class
Links
Code private, available on request

The problem

When a unit of blood moves from donor to lab to hospital to patient, every hand-off is a chance to lose track of it. A shared ledger can make that history trustworthy, but blood records are medical records, and putting personal data on a public chain would break Zimbabwe's Data Protection Act.

What I built

BloodChain uses two ledgers. All personal data lives off-chain in Supabase PostgreSQL behind row level security. On-chain, each blood bag is a token on the Hedera Token Service that carries only a cryptographic hash, so the audit trail is public while the people in it stay private.

  • Role-based workflows for blood banks, labs, hospitals and donors.
  • An Express API gateway that salts patient identifiers and manages keys.
  • Expiry tracking, QR codes for anonymous donor profiles and token-based donor incentives.
  • Twelve dated SQL migrations, including one that locks a sensitive table to the backend service only.

What happened

This was my final year project at the National University of Science and Technology, supervised by Joseph Mutengeni, and it was marked 81, a first. It also produced an IEEE-format research paper co-authored with my supervisor. Transactions confirmed in about three seconds on average, at well under a cent each.
