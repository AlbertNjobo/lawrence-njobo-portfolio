The problem

At the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Zimbabwe I replaced a paper procurement process with a Power Platform app. Approvals got faster, but every step still needed someone to push the request along to the next person.

Procurely asks a different question: what if the system did the pushing, and people only made the decisions?

What I built

You describe what you need in plain language, or say it out loud. Procurely qualifies the request, checks it against the organisation's purchasing policy, finds suitable vendors, issues requests for quotation, compares the bids and drafts the purchase order. People approve at fixed checkpoints, so nothing is bought without a human saying yes.

  • A drag-and-drop workflow designer, so each organisation can set its own steps.
  • Specialist sub-agents for parts of the job such as vendor negotiation.
  • A retrieval knowledge base: policy documents are embedded and searched with an HNSW index, so the agent cites the rule it is applying.
  • Email notifications through Resend, and speech-to-text input.

What happened

The first version, ProcurePilot, ran on Supabase with PostgreSQL, row level security and pgvector for policy search. I rebuilt it as Procurely on Firebase Firestore with a Zvec vector store, and it now runs with Docker Compose on Alibaba Cloud behind Cloudflare.

The hardest part was not the model. It was deciding where the checkpoints go, because an agent that asks for approval too often is just a slower form, and one that asks too rarely is a risk.
